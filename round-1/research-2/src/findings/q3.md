# Q3 — Calibrating the novelty of "verbalized/internal-salience disagreement → protect the sentence" for prompt compression

Hypothesis under test: for a small instruct LLM doing prompt compression on multi-hop QA
passages, DISAGREEMENT between attention-based internal sentence salience and the model's own
verbalized list of essential sentences is a content-level uncertainty signal; disagreeing
sentences should be PROTECTED (kept), validated against leave-one-sentence-out (LOSO) ablation
ground truth.

All citations below carry an arXiv id/DOI and one exact quote pulled with `fetch_grep` (verbatim
from the PDF text layer, tagged VERIFIED) unless marked UNVERIFIED.

## 3a. Verbalized vs. internal confidence — established facts + gap-as-signal search

- **Kadavath et al. 2022 (2207.05221)**, "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know."
  VERIFIED: "we investigate whether models can be trained to predict 'P(IK)', the probability
  that 'I know' the answer to a question, without reference to any particular proposed answer.
  Models perform well at predicting P(IK) and partially generalize across tasks, though they
  struggle with calibration of P(IK) on new tasks."
- **Tian et al. 2023 (2305.14975)**. VERIFIED: "verbalized confidences emitted as output tokens
  are typically better-calibrated than the model's conditional probabilities on the TriviaQA,
  SciQ, and TruthfulQA benchmarks, often reducing the expected calibration error by a relative
  50%."
- **Xiong et al. 2024 (2306.13063)**. VERIFIED: "LLMs, when verbalizing their confidence, tend
  to be overconfident, potentially imitating human patterns of expressing confidence."
- **Orgad et al. 2024/ICLR2025 (2410.02707)**, "LLMs Know More Than They Show." VERIFIED:
  "we reveal a discrepancy between LLMs' internal encoding and external behavior: they may
  encode the correct answer, yet consistently generate an incorrect one."
- **Azaria & Mitchell 2023 (2304.13734)**. VERIFIED: "our trained classifier achieves an average
  of 71% to 83% accuracy labeling which sentences are true versus false" from hidden-layer
  activations (SAPLMA).
- **Kossen et al. 2024 (2406.15927)**. VERIFIED: "We propose Semantic Entropy Probes (SEPs),
  linear probes trained on the hidden states of LLMs to capture semantic entropy."

**Key search — does anyone USE the verbalized/internal GAP as an actionable signal (abstain /
select / route), rather than just measuring it?** Closest papers found:

1. **Miao & Ungar, "Closing the Confidence-Faithfulness Gap in Large Language Models"
   (2603.25052, preprint).** VERIFIED: "reading the model's internal accuracy signal and
   steering its verbalized output to match, we are able to close much of the faithfulness gap of
   verbalized confidence." — This treats the gap as a target to *eliminate by steering the
   verbal output toward the internal one*, not as a per-item signal that is itself kept/acted on;
   there is no per-instance downstream decision (abstain/protect/route) conditioned on
   *how large* the gap is for that item.
2. **Zhang et al., "Direct Confidence Alignment" (2512.11998).** VERIFIED: "it has been observed
   that the internal confidence of a model, derived from token probabilities, is not well
   aligned with its verbalized confidence... we propose Direct Confidence Alignment (DCA)... to
   align an LLM's verbalized confidence with its internal confidence." Again the gap is a
   training target to shrink, not a per-item routing/protection signal.
3. General WebSearch synthesis (UNVERIFIED, snippets only, no single paper pinned down):
   "linear probes trained on hidden states achieve AUROC values in the 0.6–0.8 range on the
   same items where verbal confidence is near chance" — repeatedly surfaced as a generic
   framing across several 2025/2026 abstention-survey pages, suggesting the *idea* that
   verbal-vs-internal divergence per item could flag hard cases is "in the air" but I could not
   pin it to one paper that operationalizes disagreement as a per-item selective-prediction
   signal at the sentence/item level.

**Verdict for 3a:** established — verbalized and internal confidence are imperfectly correlated
and the gap is well documented; the closest operationalizations *close* the gap (steer/align
training objectives), not *use per-item gap size as a protect/abstain trigger*. No paper found
that treats disagreement between the two channels as a content-level uncertainty flag for
selective retention/dropping of sub-document units (sentences).

## 3b. Self-explanation faithfulness vs. attention/attribution — sentence-level agreement

- **Huang et al. 2023 (2310.11207)**, "Can Large Language Models Explain Themselves?"
  (title/authorship confirmed via search result "[2310.11207] Can Large Language Models Explain
  Themselves? A Study of LLM-Generated Self-Explanations"; not independently fetch_grepped this
  session — UNVERIFIED exact quote, but id/title confirmed).
- **Madsen et al. 2024 (2401.07927)** — UNVERIFIED (not directly fetched this session; referenced
  by the task as self-consistency work, task/model-dependent faithfulness).
- Directly relevant, newly found: **"Not All Explanations Simulate Equally: Comparing Verbalized
  Feature Attributions and Self-Generated Rationales" (2606.01148, 2026 preprint).** VERIFIED:
  "A highlighted sentence, a token attribution map, and a fluent self-generated rationale may all
  appear to explain a model prediction, but they may support very different kinds of
  understanding," and "Sentence-level attributions provide stronger simulatability gains than
  token-level attributions... CoT rationales provide the strongest simulation signal among the
  studied types." This paper explicitly compares sentence-level attribution-based explanations
  against self-generated (verbalized) rationales via *simulatability*, not raw agreement, and
  does not report an agreement number restricted to "which sentences the model calls essential"
  vs. attention weights specifically — it uses a perturbation-based attributor (MExGen), not raw
  attention.
- Krishna et al.'s disagreement-problem framing (below, 3c) and the general XAI literature
  (Jacovi & Goldberg 2020 faithfulness, Turpin et al. 2023 CoT unfaithfulness — task-cited,
  UNVERIFIED this session) establish that self-explanations are often unfaithful to the true
  decision process, but sentence-granularity attention-vs-verbalized-list agreement specifically
  is **not measured as a single clean number** in any paper found; it is scattered across
  token-level saliency-vs-attention comparisons (see 3c) and rationale-vs-attribution
  simulatability studies (2606.01148) rather than a direct "does the model's named important
  sentences match its own attention pattern" study.

**Verdict for 3b:** established — self-explanations and attribution/attention methods frequently
disagree, and agreement is format/granularity dependent (sentence-level extractive agreement is
reported as stronger than token-level, but this is about simulatability, not about a
model's introspective "list vs. attention" match). No paper found that directly measures
attention-vs-verbalized-sentence-list AGREEMENT at sentence granularity for multi-hop QA
passages, nor that treats disagreement there as an uncertainty signal.

## 3c. Disagreement-as-signal in XAI

- **Krishna et al. 2022/TMLR 2024 (2202.01602)**, "The Disagreement Problem in Explainable
  Machine Learning: A Practitioner's Perspective." VERIFIED: "we formalize and investigate the
  disagreement problem in explainable ML, a novel area of research that examines conflicts among
  post hoc explanation methods," and the paper's contributions are about *measuring* and
  *practitioner-resolving* disagreement (interviews, metrics, empirical disagreement rates
  across 6 explainers/4 datasets) — it does **not** propose using explanation disagreement as a
  downstream automated risk/uncertainty score or a trigger for any model action; resolution is
  discussed as a human/qualitative practice ("Resolving the Disagreement Problem in Practice: A
  Qualitative Study"), not an algorithmic flag.
- Closest thing to "disagreement → automated action" found: **"Selective Prediction from
  Agreement: A Lipschitz-Consistent Version Space Approach" (2605.02611).** VERIFIED: "The model
  therefore predicts only when the label is forced (i.e., all consistent heads agree), and
  abstains otherwise." This *is* disagreement-as-abstention-trigger, but it is classical
  version-space/ensemble-of-classification-heads disagreement (image classification, CIFAR-10/
  SVHN), not explanation-method disagreement and not LLM/text.

**Verdict for 3c:** Krishna et al. establish the disagreement problem for post-hoc explainers but
stop at measurement/qualitative resolution; no paper found operationalizes *explanation*
disagreement into an automated reliability/uncertainty score. The closest analog for
"disagreement → abstain/protect" as a mechanism comes from a different lineage entirely
(agreement-based selective prediction over classifier ensembles, 2605.02611), not from XAI
disagreement.

## 3d. Query-by-committee lineage

- **Seung, Opper & Sompolinsky 1992, "Query by Committee" (DOI 10.1145/130385.130417).**
  CONFIRMED and VERIFIED via ACM PDF: "We propose an algorithm called query by committee, in
  which a committee of students is trained on the same data set. The next query is chosen
  according to the principle of maximal disagreement," and "the degree of disagreement among a
  committee of learners can serve as an estimate of this information value [of a query]." This
  is the canonical disagreement-as-informativeness result.
- Closest LLM analog using **one model's disagreement across elicitation modes** (not a true
  multi-model ensemble): **Cho et al., "Active Instruction Tuning... Prompt Uncertainty"
  (2311.00288).** VERIFIED: "We represent the informativeness of new tasks with the disagreement
  of the current model outputs over perturbed prompts," motivated explicitly by BALD
  (Bayesian Active Learning by Disagreement). This treats disagreement of a *single* model's
  outputs across *perturbed prompts* (different elicitation instances of the same model) as a
  QBC-flavored informativeness/uncertainty signal — same lineage as the hypothesis's "two
  elicitation modes of one model," but applied to whole-task selection for instruction tuning,
  not to sentence-level content salience inside one input passage.
- Also found (not deeply verified, UNVERIFIED): SPUQ (2403.02509, perturbation-based uncertainty
  quantification) and cross-model semantic-disagreement ensembles (2604.17112) as adjacent
  "disagreement across sampled/perturbed elicitations of an LLM = uncertainty" instances.

**Verdict for 3d:** QBC lineage confirmed at the root; single-LLM, multi-elicitation-mode
disagacement-as-uncertainty is an active, populated pattern (prompt-perturbation disagreement,
self-consistency-based disagreement) — but always over the *model's final answer/task-level
output*, never over disagreement between an *internal attention distribution* and a *verbalized
list of important units* for the purpose of flagging content (sentences) to protect during
compression.

## 3e. Compression papers with heterogeneous/two signals, and disagreement-as-protection

- **MIST ("Every Token Leaves a Ripple in the Stream of Thought," Zhao, He, Zheng, Chen,
  arXiv:2608.31066, Aug 2026).** VERIFIED: MIST "defines token importance along two
  complementary axes: necessity, the drop in answer likelihood when a token's internal
  contribution is removed, and sufficiency, the gain in answer likelihood when that contribution
  alone is provided. Combining the two yields a unified importance score for pruning." Both axes
  are **model-internal** (residual-stream necessity/sufficiency via patching) — there is no
  verbalized/self-reported signal in MIST, and the two axes are **combined/averaged into one
  score**, not used to flag DISAGREEMENT between them as the actionable variable. This is the
  closest paper in spirit (two heterogeneous saliency signals for compression) but it (a) is
  CoT-token pruning, not multi-hop-passage sentence compression, (b) uses two internal signals,
  not one internal + one verbalized, and (c) fuses rather than dis-agrees.
- **Referential Dangling (Hu, Li, Fu, Zou, Tang, Li, Cao, Huang, arXiv:2608.04569, Aug 2026),**
  "Relevant but Incomplete: Referential Dangling as a Paradigm-Level Failure Mode in Hard Prompt
  Compression." VERIFIED: "independent selection can split dependent evidence pairs, retaining
  one member while deleting the other... we call the result referential dangling," with
  empirical rates "up to 60%" dangling on a shared HotpotQA bridge set and accuracy recovery of
  "29-34 percentage points" from reinserting the missing supporting paragraph. This is a
  structural/coreference-dependency failure mode of independent per-unit scoring — a different
  mechanism from the hypothesis's uncertainty-disagreement mechanism, but it is direct evidence
  that (i) independent importance-scoring of sentences/chunks under a budget is the dominant hard
  -compression paradigm and (ii) it demonstrably drops content whose importance is
  under-signaled by a single scoring channel — a complementary motivation for a second,
  disagreement-sensitive signal, though Hu et al.'s own fix is a dependency/coreference
  constraint, not a second saliency channel.
- Search for "uncertainty-aware prompt compression," "ensemble prompt compression," "disagreement
  token importance," "hybrid compression attention and LLM selection": returned only
  single-signal or fused-signal methods — attention-as-importance-sampling-weights (PIS,
  2504.16574: "Transformer attention scores can be interpreted as conditional token
  probabilities, making them a principled proxy for the ideal importance weights," but flags
  that "relying solely on attention scores may lead to the removal of important tokens... "
  prioritizing tokens with high variance in attention scores" as a fix — variance across layers,
  not disagreement with a verbalized signal), LLMLingua-family entropy-based scoring, and hybrid
  hard/soft compressors (CompactPrompt, HyCo2) that combine compression *mechanisms* (token
  pruning + phrase grouping + soft embeddings), not two independent *importance judges* whose
  cross-check is the signal. No paper found combines (a) attention-based internal salience and
  (b) the model's own verbalized list of essential sentences, and none treats their
  DISAGREEMENT (as opposed to their average/fusion) as the actionable variable.
- **Introspection/self-report of attention:** "Detecting the Disturbance: A Nuanced View of
  Introspective Abilities in LLMs" (2512.12411) and related concept-injection work (Anthropic-
  style, COLM 2026 paper at 2605.26242) show LLMs have only partial, inconsistent ability to
  report on their own internal states (UNVERIFIED exact figures this session — WebSearch snippet
  only cites "~20% accuracy" in some settings, "up to 88% vs 10% chance" in others depending on
  method; not independently fetch_grepped). This is relevant background: it implies a model's
  verbalized "essential sentence" list is not a transparent readout of its own attention, which
  is exactly why the two could diverge and carry independent information — but no paper directly
  tests this for sentence-salience-vs-verbalized-importance.

**Verdict for 3e:** No compression paper combines an internal signal with a verbalized signal and
treats their disagreement (rather than their fusion/average) as the actionable variable. The two
closest 2026 papers (MIST, Referential Dangling) both attack the single-signal /
independent-scoring weakness from different angles — MIST by fusing two *internal* signals,
Hu et al. by adding a *dependency constraint* — neither introduces a second, verbalized signal or
exploits cross-signal disagreement.

## Calibrated novelty statement

It is well established that a model's verbalized confidence and its internally encoded
confidence are imperfectly correlated (Kadavath et al. 2207.05221; Tian et al. 2305.14975;
Xiong et al. 2306.13063; Orgad et al. 2410.02707; Azaria & Mitchell 2304.13734; Kossen et al.
2406.15927), that LLM self-explanations are frequently unfaithful to the process that actually
drove the output and disagree with attribution/attention-based accounts (Krishna et al.
2202.01602's "disagreement problem"; 2606.01148's sentence-vs-token-vs-rationale simulatability
gaps), and that disagreement among elicitation modes is a classical informativeness signal
(query-by-committee, Seung/Opper/Sompolinsky 1992, DOI 10.1145/130385.130417) that has already
been re-instantiated for single LLMs as disagreement-across-perturbed-prompts (2311.00288) and
disagreement-across-classifier-heads triggering abstention (2605.02611). The closest existing
operationalization is Miao & Ungar's and Zhang et al.'s work on the verbalized/internal
confidence gap (2603.25052; 2512.11998), but both treat the gap as a training target to *close*
(steer/align the verbal channel to the internal one), never as a per-item signal that gates a
downstream retain/drop decision; and the closest compression-specific analog, MIST
(arXiv:2608.31066), fuses two *internal* saliency axes (necessity, sufficiency) into one score
rather than cross-checking an internal signal against a verbalized one. No paper found combines
an attention-based internal salience signal with a model's own verbalized list of essential
sentences and treats their point-of-DISAGREEMENT — rather than their average, their fusion, or
the size of a scalar confidence gap in isolation — as the mechanism that decides which specific
content units survive compression, and none validates such a mechanism against leave-one-
sentence-out ablation ground truth. Verdict: **no direct prior claim found; the closest prior
claim is the verbalized/internal confidence-gap literature (2603.25052, 2512.11998) plus MIST's
dual-internal-signal fusion for compression (2608.31066), and the hypothesis adds (a) using a
*verbalized* signal (not just probes) as one of the two channels, (b) operating at
sentence/content-unit granularity for compression rather than scalar confidence or CoT-token
pruning, and (c) treating cross-signal DISAGREEMENT itself (not fusion or gap-closing) as the
protect/drop decision rule, validated against LOSO ablation ground truth.**
